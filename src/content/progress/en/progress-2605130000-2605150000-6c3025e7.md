Another episode on the slow road to an editor that stays small on the surface and stubbornly useful underneath — we spent this stretch not adding *more* power, but making the power already there feel less like a negotiation.

The export sheet was doing its job, but it looked like it was borrowing someone else’s clothes. So we gave it a little **makeup**: inputs that belong with the rest of the app, a quality slider with real weight, a toggle that actually *toggles* instead of whispering in checkbox, and — because browsers guard native `<select>` styling the way castle walls once guarded heavy scrollbars — a **custom dropdown** that finally sits where we want it in the layout.

That part felt like victory until typing started to feel wrong. Each keystroke arrived like an elephant parade: one. character. at. a. time. We wanted **cheetah**, not pachyderm. The culprit was a pile of tiny state foot-guns — enough that fixing them turned into a quiet therapy session with the global store. Persistence had been flaky enough to notice in the margins: saves that didn’t always land, moments where the UI hesitated like it was catching its breath. Tightening that path wasn’t glamorous, but it’s the kind of work that makes “advanced amateur” sessions survivable: you’re not here to babysit the app while you chase a crop.

Then, while wiring export up to collections, an older path woke us with a slap: **Move Selected** had gone shy. First bug in a while that felt like a *systems smell* — the sort that whispers: *maybe you want tests here eventually*. Not today, friend-monkey emoji implied; today we just fixed the thing and moved on. Along the way we added path and folder-name rules that behave on a few OSes’ lists — including Windows, which remains a friendly myth on our machines for now.

Last pass: **grid sanding** — not a headline feature, but the kind of small friction removal that photographers notice in their fingers after ten minutes.

North star unchanged: we’re still avoiding the endless pro-suite bloat. The win is when export feels obvious, typing feels instant, and “move my selection” works without apology — because serious hobbyists live in those boring loops every day, and the app should get out of the way.
