In this early stretch, fotonoza-app stopped being a fantasy folder on disk and became a thing you could open in a browser—still hungry for photos, already opinionated about layout. Same compass as ever: useful without pretending to be the whole universe.

The **init** commit was the polite knock at the door: React, Vite, Tailwind, TypeScript, a shy **Tauri** harness muttering “desktop someday,” plus domain types and a **Zustand** brain so state would have somewhere dignified to live. Early **EN/PL** strings and a tiny **i18n** helper already hinted at who we’re building for—people who notice language and tone, not just sliders.

Then **setup layout** landed, and suddenly I wasn’t staring at a tutorial page—I was staring at *chrome*. A **TopBar** with undo/redo and “pick some JPEGs,” **View** vs **Edit** like two doors into the same modest workshop, a **WorkingArea** that knows how to zoom and pan before it knows how to *mean* anything artistically, and panels that beg to be resized because that’s how serious hobbyists actually work: muscle memory, elbows on the desk, “give me more histogram, less timeline.” The center still asks, softly, to **Import from device**; the left rail already carries a **history** whisper; the bottom strip labels itself **Timeline** like it’s reserving a seat for the filmstrip that shows up once photos exist; the right rail waits in the dark until **Edit** mode, when **HistogramCanvas** and a cheeky “+ Exposure” placeholder admit we’re choosing our battles—**color sense before feature glut**.

Not a Lightroom costume party—just the ~20% shell where advanced-amateurs can breathe: room for crops and color without a billing-by-the-hour control surface. It feels a little empty on purpose. Empty is honest. Empty is permission to say **no** until the next knob truly earns its rent.
