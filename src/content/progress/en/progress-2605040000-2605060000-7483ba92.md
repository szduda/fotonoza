Episode… honestly, who’s counting anymore? “Small app, big evenings” — this round the viewer grew *opinions about light*: less changelog, more someone finally flipped the breaker.

This batch is messy in the best way: ESLint nagging us into adulthood, **`zundo`** and **`immer`** turning undo/redo from a vibe into something you can trust, stores splitting apart like a tired LEGO castle (“fine, histogram, you get your own drawer”), Rust controllers getting names instead of one endless `lib.rs` scroll, and the working area finally admitting it has two moods — **edit** and **view** — instead of pretending one layout can serve both gods.

But the emotional headline — the one that made me grin at the screen like an idiot — is embarrassingly simple: **exposure**, **contrast**, **saturation**. Not curves. Not twelve masked adjustment layers. Not a faux-Lightroom panic room. Just the three sliders advanced-amateurs actually reach for when a photo is *almost* right: lift the shadows without apologizing, wake the midtones up, decide whether the world should feel juicy or restrained. Still the same bargain: **depth without bloat**, usefulness without a certification course.

And yes, there’s infrastructure glitter too — a tidier top bar, buttons that behave, export progress that doesn’t ghost you, Polish strings getting love, macOS notes for humans who forget which gatekeeper mood they’re in — because polish is how seriousness sneaks in without turning into enterprise soup.

If you squint at a fresh preview build, you’ll see the shell we’ve been carving: chrome edges you can breathe in, thumbnails and timeline keeping you honest on the left, the photo holding court in the middle, and on the right — beside the histogram’s judgement — those three sliders, quietly daring you to make *one more* small tweak before you call it a night. That’s the fantasy we’re chasing: not “pro suite,” just **the twenty percent that survives real life**… with undo when you inevitably overshoot saturation like a caffeinated gremlin.

Warmly, stubbornly, still building.
