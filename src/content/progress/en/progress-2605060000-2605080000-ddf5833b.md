Still refusing the Lightroom cosplay: we want the **~20% of knobs** serious hobbyists actually touch, done well enough that you stop thinking about the software.

Over this stretch the app picked up a pile of changes that sound technical on paper but read as *relief* in the chair. The timeline — that stubborn filmstrip at the bottom — started carrying **real thumbnails**, generated with help from the Tauri side so we’re not faking the library with gray placeholders. Scrolling a shoot finally feels like remembering which frame had the good light, not like auditing a spreadsheet.

Upstairs, the **histogram** got replaced and then chased for performance. For people who tweak exposure and color by *feel*, the histogram isn’t decoration; it’s a honest little lie detector. When it drags, you stop trusting it — so making it snappier is less “optimization” and more “keep the feedback loop human-sized.”

The **effects surface** got some love too: layout tightened, basic adjustments and shadows/highlights got room to breathe, and there’s more structure peeking through for the Darktable-backed pieces without turning the right rail into a space station. The **GPU preview canvas** kept evolving underneath, because nothing in this story ages faster than “it looked fine until I moved the slider.”

The snapshot that freezes this diary beat is the kind of patch note writers reach for when a week has been messy: **perf**, **timeline**, **simplify the top bar**, **cleanup**. That’s the emotional arc: fewer chrome excuses, more of the photo. Pause a preview there and you’d plausibly see a calmer strip of controls up top, a bottom timeline that actually shows your frames, a quicker histogram riding shotgun, and the edit stack still narrow enough that you’re not lost — just deep enough that an advanced amateur can stay curious.

The win isn’t *more* panels—it’s when the strip and the scopes stop picking fights with you, and you can ask the frame better questions again.
