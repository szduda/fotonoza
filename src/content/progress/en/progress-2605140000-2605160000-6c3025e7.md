Another beat on the slow road to an editor that *feels* small on the surface but still respects people who actually care about color, crops, and the moment before they send a photo out the door.

The export modal is one of those places where “simple” can lie to you. On paper it is just a few choices: where, how big, how sharp, whether metadata tags along for the ride. In practice it is where hesitation lives — the last gate before a file becomes *real* outside your tab. So we went at it like a vanity pass on a film shoot: fresher inputs that borrow the same DNA as the filter panel, a quality slider with a little more **heft** in the thumb, a proper toggle where a lonely checkbox used to shrug, and — because browsers guard `select` styling the way they once guarded chonky scrollbars — a **custom dropdown** that finally sits in the family photo with the rest of the UI.

Beauty work is never just skin. Somewhere under the fresh paint, state was doing something cruel: every keystroke plodded like an elephant parade when we want the **cheetah sprint** you expect from a responsive tool. Tracing that down was the sort of bug that makes you whisper apologies to your past self. And while we were in the gnarly bits, persistence — the quiet worker that remembers your world between visits — had been *occasionally* failing to show up on time, leaving little interface stutters hanging around like uninvited guests. Tightening that up is the opposite of a headline feature; it is the kind of fix advanced-amateurs notice as relief rather than fireworks.

While wiring export thinking into collections, “Move Selected” decided to play dead — a polite first flag that we will eventually need **feature testing** with teeth, even if “not today” remains a sacred phrase on a tiny team. The actual fix came with a side of pragmatism: validating file and directory names with an eye on the weird rules different operating systems enforce, **Windows** included, even if that platform still lives in the “here be dragons” corner of our testing map. A little grid sanding at the end, because nothing says “we are serious about craft” like making the boring rectangle behave.

We are still saying **no** to becoming a billing-by-the-hour retouching suite. But saying no only works if the yes-es *land cleanly* — export is one of those yes-es, and it is allowed to look sharp while it works hard.
