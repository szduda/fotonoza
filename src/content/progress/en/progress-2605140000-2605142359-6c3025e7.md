Another stretch on the slow road to an editor that stays *small on purpose* but doesn’t feel cheap: the kind of tool serious hobbyists actually open every week, not a rental suite for people who bill by the hour.

This episode started with vanity — in the best way. The **export** sheet was due for makeup. So the inputs leaned into the same visual language as the filter side of the house: a thicker quality **slider** you can actually grab, a proper **toggle** where a shy little checkbox used to hide, and a **custom dropdown** because the browser still treats `< select >` like a state secret, same energy as those chunky scrollbars we used to fight. It *looks* like the rest of the app now, which matters when export is the last thing you see before you post.

Beauty came with physics problems. For a while every keystroke landed with the dignity of an elephant parade — fine for a parade, terrible for filenames and options. The culprit was state thrashing and work happening where it shouldn’t; chasing that down felt less like “optimization” and more like **unsticking a sticky drawer**. Related gremlins in the global store meant persistence occasionally *forgot its lines* and the UI hiccupped when nobody asked it to. Tightening that path is the boring stuff readers don’t cheer for — until it simply *stops happening*, and then it’s the whole afternoon’s win.

While wiring export to **collections**, a nastier flag waved: **Move Selected** had gone walkabout. Not a philosophical crisis — a practical one, and honestly a nudge that *real* feature tests are on the horizon. Not today’s battle; today was surgical: make move work again, and clamp filenames and folder names so each OS in the list (yes, even the one on the list we haven’t sacrified a VM to yet) gets characters it can live with.

Then a little ending ritual: **final grid sanding** — not a headline feature, just the last pass where spacing stops *nagging* and the canvas stops feeling like it’s arguing with you. That’s the north star in miniature: fewer knobs, sharper edges where it counts, and the quiet relief when the app quits auditioning for Photoshop and just does its job.
