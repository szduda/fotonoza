This stretch felt less like “shipping features” and more like **finally getting room to breathe**—small on purpose, but not toy-small.

We spent a lot of cycles on the unglamorous stuff that advanced-amateurs quietly demand: a timeline that doesn’t fight you, thumbnails that don’t melt your machine, and a split between **view** and **edit** that mirrors how people actually work (browse → commit → tweak → back out). Keyboard stepping through the filmstrip, gentler transitions, **virtualization** and thumbnail jobs getting smarter—none of that photographs well for a marketing page, but it’s the difference between “cool demo” and “I can live in this for an hour.”

Then the fun parts showed up, almost in a pile: **filters** that make a growing library feel searchable instead of hoarded; a **metadata** panel that answers the boring questions without turning the app into a spreadsheet; **collections** (plus the little dignity fixes like handling **rating 0** like a real zero, not a glitch); export progress that doesn’t leave you guessing; even a **splash screen** because sometimes the first second should feel intentional, not apologetic. History for effects landed too—small, but it chips away at the fear of “ruining” an image when you’re really just exploring.

Under the hood we kept wrestling the preview pipeline until it behaved: the **GPU canvas** had a moment where it simply… refused to show the picture (the kind of bug that makes you stare at shaders like they owe you money). Pulling **histogram** concerns into a cleaner lane helped the UI stop lying about what you’re doing to tones—and sets us up to trust color moves the way hobbyists do: by eye, with guardrails.

By the end of this batch, the canvas gets a tool that’s very *us*: **focus peaking / highlights marking**, the kind of assist that says “we’re not building a retoucher’s invoice machine, but we *do* care whether you nailed sharpness and whether your highlights are giggling.” Pair that with **panel layout** tightening so the chrome stops stealing pixels from the photo, and the whole thing starts to feel like one coherent desk: library on the left, story along the bottom, truth in the middle.

Plenty of hydras out there. We’re simply pickier about which heads earn a meal.
